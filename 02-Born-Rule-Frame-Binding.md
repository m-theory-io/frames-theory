# The Born Rule as a Frame-Binding Measure in Frames Theory

William J. House

## Abstract

The Born rule is usually read as a probability rule for measurement outcomes: if a quantum system is prepared in the state $|\psi\rangle=\sum_k c_k |k\rangle$, then outcome $k$ is observed with probability $P(k)=|c_k|^2$. This paper keeps the mathematical content of the Born rule intact while changing its ontological interpretation. In Frames Theory, the Born rule is not the probability that one globally real outcome is selected from unreal alternatives. It is the measure governing how a quantum phenomenon becomes informationally bound to a receiving frame. Binding is preceded by propagation: an informational structure produced by a generating frame becomes available to candidate receiving frames, only some of which can stabilize it. At the quantum-physical level, such binding begins as entangling correlation between a system and a receiving frame, but stable frame-binding also requires decoherent stabilization and informational accessibility. The quantity $|c_k|^2$ is therefore interpreted as a frame-relative binding measure, denoted $\mu_F(k)$, for the outcome-relative state $k$ with respect to a frame $F$. This preserves the empirical role of the Born rule while locating probability within frame-relative actualization rather than universal collapse.

## 1. Introduction

Quantum theory contains two ideas that sit uneasily beside one another. The first is unitary evolution: isolated quantum systems evolve continuously and linearly. The second is outcome definiteness: measurements yield determinate results. Between these two ideas stands the Born rule, which tells us how to assign probabilities to possible outcomes.

In the standard formulation, if a system is in the state

$$
|\psi\rangle = \sum_k c_k |k\rangle,
$$

then the probability of observing outcome $k$ is

$$
P(k)=|c_k|^2.
$$

This rule is among the most successful laws in physics. Its numerical predictions are not in question here. The question is ontological: what is this probability a probability of?

In collapse-based language, the Born rule is often treated as the probability that the wavefunction collapses to one outcome rather than another. In many-worlds or Everettian approaches, the same expression is interpreted through branch weights, rational credence, or decision-theoretic constraints. In relational approaches, outcome facts are not absolute but relative to the systems involved. Each of these strategies preserves the operational success of the Born rule, but each gives a different account of what probability means.

Frames Theory offers another interpretation. It treats observation as an informational relation between frames rather than as the appearance of a globally privileged outcome. A measurement does not require the universe to select one absolute result. Rather, a receiving frame becomes informationally bound to an outcome-relative state. From within that frame, the outcome is definite. From a broader or differently situated frame, the interaction may still be represented unitarily.

The central claim of this paper is therefore:

> The Born rule quantifies the distribution of informational binding events between quantum phenomena and receiving frames.

The Born rule remains mathematically unchanged. What changes is its meaning. The quantity $|c_k|^2$ is not interpreted as the chance that one globally real outcome is selected. It is interpreted as the measure by which outcome $k$ becomes actualized relative to a receiving frame $F$.

We write this as

$$
\mu_F(k)=|c_k|^2,
$$

where $\mu_F(k)$ is the frame-binding measure for outcome $k$ relative to frame $F$.

This paper does not claim a new first-principles derivation of the Born rule. Its aim is more conservative and, for the purposes of Frames Theory, more immediate: to give Born probabilities a precise ontological role in a no-universal-collapse framework.

## 2. Frames, Observation, and Binding

Frames Theory begins from the idea that physical description is always situated within an informational context. A frame is a bounded informational context with an internal state and relations to other frames. A frame may be a measuring apparatus, an organism, a laboratory, an observer, an environment, or any physical system capable of receiving, preserving, and propagating information.

Observation is not identified with consciousness. It is a directed informational interaction. When one system becomes correlated with another in a way that constrains its subsequent state, an observation-like relation has occurred. A photodetector, a Stern-Gerlach apparatus, a notebook, and a human observer may all function as receiving frames in this sense.

For the present paper, several notions are central.

**Generating frame.** A generating frame is the frame in which the relevant quantum phenomenon, interaction, or informational structure originates.

**Informational signal.** An informational signal is the structure or constraint made physically available by a generating frame. The word "signal" does not imply an intentional message, a conscious recipient, or a preselected addressee. The question is not "signal who?" but "which frames are physically capable of receiving and stabilizing this information?"

**Candidate binding site.** A candidate binding site is a frame whose state-space, coupling, and contextual constraints make it capable of receiving and stabilizing the propagated signal.

**Compatibility check.** A compatibility check is the physical filtering by which a candidate frame either can or cannot incorporate the propagated signal. This may involve coupling strength, basis alignment, environmental conditions, threshold effects, or the availability of stable record states.

**Receiving frame.** A receiving frame $F$ is a frame that becomes informationally correlated with a quantum system through interaction.

**Entangling correlation.** At the quantum level, a system and receiving frame become entangled when their joint state is no longer factorizable. This is the microscopic substrate from which quantum frame-binding begins.

**Binding.** Binding is the process by which a receiving frame becomes correlated with an outcome-relative state.

**Decoherent frame-binding.** Decoherent frame-binding is binding that has been stabilized by environmental propagation, loss of accessible interference, and the availability of persistent record states.

**Redundant frame-binding.** Redundant frame-binding occurs when the same outcome-relative information is stabilized across multiple nested or neighboring frames. This is how local facts become practically objective across a frame network.

**Actualization.** Actualization is the result of binding from within the receiving frame. An outcome is actual for a frame when that frame has become informationally stabilized with respect to the corresponding outcome-relative state.

These definitions separate ideas that are often blurred together. Propagation makes information available to possible receiving frames. Entangling correlation supplies the microscopic quantum correlation. Binding is the successful relational process by which one of those frames incorporates an outcome-relative state. Decoherence and accessibility stabilize the relation enough for it to function as a record. Actualization is the frame-relative result. Collapse, in this view, is not a universal physical jump. It is the way a binding event may appear from within a frame that has become definite with respect to an outcome.

This distinction is important because it lets Frames Theory preserve both unitary evolution and local definiteness. A broader frame may describe a system-plus-apparatus interaction as a superposition of correlated terms. The receiving frame, however, is not in an indeterminate experiential or informational condition relative to itself. It is bound to a particular outcome-relative state.

Thus, Frames Theory does not deny the reality of definite outcomes. It denies that definiteness must be absolute in order to be real.

## 3. Measurement in Frames Theory

Consider a quantum system $S$ prepared in the state

$$
|\psi\rangle_S = \sum_k c_k |k\rangle_S.
$$

Let $F$ be a receiving frame initially in state $|F_0\rangle$. In an idealized measurement model, $F$ represents a frame that has already become a suitable candidate binding site for the relevant interaction. Before interaction, the joint state is

$$
|\psi\rangle_S |F_0\rangle
=
\left(\sum_k c_k |k\rangle_S\right)|F_0\rangle.
$$

An ideal measurement-like interaction correlates each system state $|k\rangle_S$ with a corresponding frame state $|F_k\rangle$:

$$
|k\rangle_S |F_0\rangle \longrightarrow |k\rangle_S |F_k\rangle.
$$

By linearity, the total state becomes

$$
|\Psi\rangle_{SF}
=
\sum_k c_k |k\rangle_S |F_k\rangle.
$$

This is the entangling stage of quantum frame-binding. The system and receiving frame are no longer separable:

$$
|\Psi\rangle_{SF}\neq |\psi\rangle_S|\phi\rangle_F.
$$

Entanglement alone, however, is not yet full frame-relative actualization. It can be microscopic, fragile, and reversible. For a binding to become a stable outcome for a receiving frame, the correlation must be accessible to that frame and typically stabilized through decoherence and environmental propagation.

This equation compresses several physical stages into one clean mathematical transformation. In a more complete frame description, an informational structure is generated, propagated through a frame network, filtered by candidate receiving frames, and only then stabilized as a binding. The notation $F$ in the idealized equation therefore represents a successful receiving frame, not every frame exposed to the propagated structure.

In conventional collapse language, one says that one outcome $k$ is observed and that the wavefunction collapses to the corresponding term. In Frames Theory, the same interaction is described differently. The receiving frame $F$ becomes informationally bound to an outcome-relative state $F_k$. The global expression need not be replaced by a single term in every possible frame of description.

This gives a different role to the Born weight. The term $|c_k|^2$ is not the probability that a universal state of reality has selected $k$ and erased the other possibilities. It is the measure associated with the binding of $F$ to the outcome-relative state $k$.

The formal replacement is simple:

$$
P(k)=|c_k|^2
$$

becomes, ontologically,

$$
\mu_F(k)=|c_k|^2.
$$

The mathematics is unchanged. The interpretation is relocated.

## 4. Propagation Before Binding

The measurement equation above is useful because it captures the correlation structure of an ideal measurement. But it should not be read as saying that any exposed frame automatically binds to an outcome. Binding is preceded by propagation.

A generating frame first makes an informational structure physically available. This may be a quantum system entering an apparatus, a microscopic event producing a detector response, or a local interaction creating a possible record. That structure then propagates through a network of frames. Some propagation is upward, from the generating frame into parent or container frames. Some is lateral, spreading into external frames coupled to the event.

The process can be described schematically:

1. **Generation:** a frame produces or contains the relevant quantum event or informational structure.
2. **Candidate coupling:** nearby, enclosing, or coupled frames become possible interaction partners.
3. **Entangling correlation:** a system and candidate receiving frame become non-factorizably correlated.
4. **Propagation:** the resulting informational structure spreads through environmental and container frames.
5. **Compatibility:** the candidate frame must be able to register, stabilize, or preserve the relevant distinction.
6. **Decoherent stabilization:** environmental records suppress accessible interference and make the binding robust.
7. **Actualization:** the outcome becomes definite relative to the receiving frame.

This gives a clearer answer to the question of what a signal is signaling. It is not necessarily signaling to a person, agent, or preassigned observer. It is propagating as a physical availability condition through a structured network. The relevant question is which frames can become candidate binding sites.

In a nested system, a detector event may first become available within the detector frame, then propagate into the apparatus frame, then into the laboratory environment, then into an observer's sensory and cognitive frame, and then into a notebook, database, or shared scientific record. These are not all the same binding event. They are linked stabilizations across a propagation network.

Nor does every propagated informational structure bind everywhere it travels. A frame may be exposed to a signal without being able to register it. It may lack the relevant coupling, the relevant basis, the relevant memory states, or the relevant contextual conditions. Propagation creates possible binding sites. It does not by itself produce actualization.

## 5. The Frame-Binding Measure

The frame-binding measure $\mu_F(k)$ is the measure assigned to the possible successful binding of a receiving frame $F$ to outcome $k$, once $F$ has become a candidate binding site for the relevant outcome context. It is not a measure of mere exposure to a propagated signal. For a normalized quantum state, it satisfies

$$
\sum_k \mu_F(k)=1,
$$

with

$$
\mu_F(k) \geq 0
$$

for every $k$.

In ordinary quantum cases, Frames Theory identifies this measure with the Born weight:

$$
\mu_F(k)=|c_k|^2.
$$

This definition should not be read as introducing a new numerical prediction. It is a reinterpretation of an existing rule. The Born rule already tells us the distribution of observed outcomes. Frames Theory says that what is being distributed is not universal selection or mere signal exposure, but successful frame-relative binding.

The distinction matters. If probability is treated as a property of global collapse, then one must explain when collapse occurs, what counts as a measuring event, and how a single outcome becomes absolute without contradicting unitary dynamics. If probability is treated as a frame-binding measure, then the relevant event is not a universal rupture in the wavefunction but the establishment of an informational relation between a system and a receiving frame. Propagation determines which frames may become eligible for such a relation; the Born measure weights the possible bindings once the relevant outcome context is in place.

This also changes the meaning of alternatives. The alternatives in the superposition are not unreal in the simple sense of being nonexistent. Nor are they all equally actual relative to every frame. Rather, they represent possible outcome-relative bindings. Once a frame is bound to one outcome-relative state, that outcome is actual for that frame. Other terms are not actualized relative to that frame, though they may remain part of a broader unitary description.

In this way, the Born rule becomes a rule for frame-relative actualization.

## 6. Local Definiteness Without Universal Collapse

The central advantage of the frame-binding interpretation is that it separates local definiteness from universal collapse.

Suppose the system and frame evolve into

$$
|\Psi\rangle_{SF}
=
\sum_k c_k |k\rangle_S |F_k\rangle.
$$

A frame external to the interaction may describe the joint system as a superposition of correlated states. But for the receiving frame associated with $F_k$, the informational state is definite with respect to $k$. The frame does not need access to a global God's-eye description in order for its outcome to be actual. Actuality is indexed to the frame in which the binding occurs.

This allows Frames Theory to say three things at once:

1. The global description need not undergo a non-unitary collapse.
2. The receiving frame can nevertheless have a definite outcome.
3. The Born rule measures the distribution of possible bindings between the quantum system and the receiving frame.

This is not the claim that collapse never appears in any description. It is the narrower and more defensible claim that collapse is not a universal physical event. A frame-relative description may use collapse-like language as an effective description of local stabilization. But the underlying ontology does not require a single absolute collapse imposed on all frames.

On this view, measurement is not the moment when reality becomes definite everywhere. It is the moment when definiteness is established within a receiving frame.

Local definiteness can then become more robust as the bound information propagates into additional frames. A detector may first stabilize an outcome-relative state. That stabilized state may then be taken up by an apparatus frame, a laboratory frame, an observer frame, a written or digital record, and finally a broader environmental or social frame. The resulting record is not produced by every frame instantly sharing an absolute collapse. It is produced by repeated compatible binding across a network of nested and external frames.

This gives a compact account of objectivity: objectivity is redundant frame-binding across a recursive information network. The more widely an outcome-relative correlation is decoherently stabilized and made accessible to compatible frames, the more objective it becomes for that network.

The phrase "outcome actualization" should therefore be understood carefully. It does not mean that a previously unreal global outcome becomes the only real state of the universe. It means that a frame has entered a stable informational relation with one outcome-relative state. Actualization is local, relational, and physically grounded in informational correlation.

## 7. Why the Born Form Is Natural

This paper does not attempt a full derivation of the Born rule. Still, Frames Theory can explain why the Born form is the natural candidate for a quantum binding measure.

A binding measure should satisfy several constraints.

First, it should be normalized. If the possible outcome-relative bindings for a frame are exhaustive, their measures should sum to one:

$$
\sum_k \mu_F(k)=1.
$$

Second, it should be positive. A possible binding cannot have negative measure:

$$
\mu_F(k)\geq 0.
$$

Third, it should be insensitive to global and local phase choices that do not alter observable outcome structure. If

$$
c_k \longrightarrow e^{i\theta_k}c_k,
$$

then the binding measure associated with the outcome should not change merely because the amplitude has been phase-rotated. The expression $|c_k|^2$ satisfies this immediately.

Fourth, it should be additive over mutually exclusive alternatives. If a receiving frame cannot be bound to both $i$ and $j$ in the same outcome context, then the measure of the coarse-grained alternative $i \lor j$ should be the sum of the measures assigned to $i$ and $j$:

$$
\mu_F(i \lor j)=\mu_F(i)+\mu_F(j).
$$

Fifth, it should be consistent under coarse-graining. If several fine-grained outcomes are later treated as one macroscopic outcome, the binding measure assigned to the macroscopic outcome should equal the sum of the corresponding fine-grained measures.

These constraints do not by themselves constitute a complete theorem. But they clarify what kind of quantity the Born rule must be in a frame-binding interpretation. It must be a normalized, positive, phase-invariant, additive measure over possible frame-relative actualizations. For ordinary quantum amplitudes, the Born weight $|c_k|^2$ is exactly such a quantity.

Thus, Frames Theory can accept standard derivations of the Born rule, such as Gleason-type arguments, symmetry-based arguments, or decision-theoretic arguments, while reinterpreting their conclusion. These derivations constrain the admissible measure. Frames Theory specifies what that measure is a measure of.

It is a measure of binding.

## 8. Relation to Other Interpretations

The frame-binding interpretation overlaps with several existing approaches but is identical to none of them.

### Everettian or Many-Worlds Interpretations

Frames Theory agrees with Everettian approaches that universal collapse is unnecessary. The joint state may continue to evolve unitarily, and the appearance of a single outcome can be understood without postulating a global physical jump.

The difference is emphasis. Everettian interpretations often explain probability through branch weights, rational credence, or decision-theoretic constraints. Frames Theory instead places probability at the site of informational binding. The question is not primarily, "Which branch should an observer expect to find themselves in?" It is, "With what measure does a receiving frame become bound to each outcome-relative state?"

This avoids making branch ontology the central interpretive object. The frame is the central object.

### Decoherence and Envariance

Decoherence explains why certain outcome bases become stable and why interference between macroscopic alternatives becomes effectively inaccessible. Envariance-based arguments, especially those associated with Zurek, attempt to derive Born weights from symmetries of entangled quantum states.

Frames Theory is compatible with these ideas. Decoherence can help explain why certain frame states $|F_k\rangle$ are stable enough to function as outcome records. Envariance and related symmetry arguments may help constrain the form of the admissible measure.

But Frames Theory adds a distinct interpretive claim: the Born weight is the measure of frame-binding. Decoherence may explain stability. Symmetry may constrain the numerical form. Frame-binding explains what probability is about.

### Relational Quantum Mechanics

Relational Quantum Mechanics holds that physical properties are relative to interacting systems rather than absolute. Frames Theory shares this rejection of a frame-independent catalog of all facts.

The difference lies in the structure of the relational network. Frames Theory emphasizes bounded informational contexts, recursive containment, receiving frames, and the propagation of consistency between frames. A measurement is not merely a binary relation between two systems. It is an event in a broader network of informational contexts.

The Born rule, on this view, is not just a probability rule for relational facts. It is the measure governing the binding of outcome-relative information into a receiving frame.

### QBism

QBism treats quantum probabilities as expressions of an agent's personal degrees of belief about future experiences. Frames Theory shares the idea that quantum probabilities are not simple descriptions of observer-independent collapse events.

However, Frames Theory does not reduce frames to conscious agents or subjective expectations. A frame is a physical and informational structure. A detector, apparatus, lab, or environment can function as a receiving frame. Probability is therefore not merely an agent's belief. It is a measure over possible physical bindings between quantum phenomena and frames.

### Objective Collapse Theories

Objective collapse theories modify or supplement unitary quantum dynamics so that physical collapse occurs under specified conditions. Frames Theory moves in the opposite direction. It does not introduce a new collapse mechanism. It explains outcome definiteness as frame-relative actualization produced by informational binding.

The Born rule is therefore not a law governing when the universe jumps. It is a law governing the measure of possible bindings within measurement-like interactions.

## 9. What the Interpretation Explains

The frame-binding interpretation gives direct answers to four central questions.

First: what does probability mean?

Probability is the measure of possible informational bindings between a quantum system and a receiving frame. In a measurement context, $\mu_F(k)=|c_k|^2$ gives the measure associated with frame $F$ becoming bound to outcome $k$.

Second: what is collapse?

Collapse is not a universal physical event. It is an effective frame-relative description of local stabilization. From within a receiving frame, one outcome is actual. From a broader frame, the system-plus-frame interaction may still be represented by a unitary superposition of correlated states.

Third: why can outcome definiteness be local without violating unitary evolution?

Because definiteness belongs to the frame-relative informational state, not to an absolute collapse of the global state. The receiving frame is definite relative to its binding. The broader state need not be reduced in every frame of description.

Fourth: how does an outcome become a stable record across multiple frames?

Through propagation and repeated compatible binding. A generating frame or initially bound receiving frame makes an informational structure available to surrounding and enclosing frames. Candidate frames that can register and stabilize the structure become further binding sites. A record becomes robust when the same outcome-relative information is repeatedly stabilized across detector, apparatus, observer, environmental, and documentary frames.

This also suggests why the Born rule is so deeply tied to measurement. It is not a generic metaphysical lottery. It applies where a quantum phenomenon can become bound to a receiving frame in one of several outcome-relative ways.

## 10. Limits of the Present Paper

The present paper is intentionally limited.

It does not provide a new Gleason-style theorem. It does not prove from first principles that all admissible frame-binding measures must take the Born form. It does not solve the preferred-basis problem by itself. It does not specify a universal dynamical law for every possible frame update, nor does it yet provide a full propagation law for informational structures moving through a frame network.

Instead, it gives the Born rule a role inside Frames Theory. It says what Born probability means once one rejects universal collapse but retains frame-relative outcome definiteness.

Future work should address at least six questions.

First, can the Born form be derived from explicit frame-consistency axioms? Such axioms might include normalization, additivity, phase invariance, coarse-graining consistency, and compatibility with unitary evolution.

Second, how should propagation dynamics be formalized across nested, lateral, and external frame networks?

Third, how should the frame-update map be specified for non-ideal measurements, weak measurements, and noisy environments?

Fourth, how do multiple receiving frames reconcile their locally actualized outcomes into stable shared records?

Fifth, what compatibility conditions determine whether a frame becomes a candidate binding site for a propagated signal?

Sixth, how does the frame-binding measure generalize to quantum field theoretic contexts, where particle number, localization, and measurement basis are themselves more subtle?

These are not defects in the present interpretation. They define the next stage of the research program.

## 11. Conclusion

The Born rule is usually stated as

$$
P(k)=|c_k|^2.
$$

Frames Theory preserves this rule but reinterprets its ontological meaning. The Born rule does not describe the probability that one globally real outcome is selected from unreal alternatives. It describes the measure by which quantum phenomena become informationally bound to receiving frames.

The central expression is therefore

$$
\mu_F(k)=|c_k|^2.
$$

Here $\mu_F(k)$ is the binding measure for outcome $k$ relative to frame $F$. It tells us how possible outcome-relative states are weighted in the process of frame-relative actualization.

This interpretation preserves the empirical content of the Born rule, avoids universal collapse, and gives outcome definiteness a local but physically meaningful status. It also clarifies the role of probability in Frames Theory: probability is not a measure of reality choosing one absolute history. It is a measure of informational binding within a network of frames.

The Born rule, then, is not merely a computational rule appended to quantum mechanics. In Frames Theory, it is the quantitative structure of frame-relative actualization.
