# Classical Objectivity as Redundant Frame-Binding in Frames Theory

## Abstract

Quantum measurement produces outcomes that are definite for receiving frames, yet ordinary experience and scientific practice require more than private definiteness. Classical facts appear public, stable, repeatable, and accessible to many observers and records. This paper develops the Frames Theory account of that transition. In Frames Theory, classical objectivity does not require a universal collapse event or a frame-independent selection of one absolute outcome. It emerges when outcome-relative information becomes redundantly stabilized across a network of compatible frames. At the microscopic level, a quantum event begins with entangling correlation between a system and a receiving frame. Decoherence and environmental propagation then stabilize some correlations while making others inaccessible. When the same outcome-relative information is recorded across detector, apparatus, environment, observer, and documentary frames, the result is a classical fact for that network. The central claim is that objectivity is redundant frame-binding across a recursive information network. This paper connects Frames Theory to decoherence and quantum Darwinism while preserving its distinctive vocabulary of propagation, binding, actualization, accessibility, containment, and consistency.

## 1. Introduction

The first four papers in this sequence develop Frames Theory as a relational informational framework for quantum measurement. The first paper introduces frames, observation, containment, and consistency. The second interprets the Born rule as a frame-binding measure. The third reinterprets projection, partial trace, and decoherence as frame-relative state update operations. The fourth asks why the frame-binding measure should take the quadratic Born form.

Those papers explain how an outcome can be definite for a receiving frame without requiring universal collapse. But a further question remains:

> How can frame-relative actualization give rise to classical objectivity?

This question matters because the world of ordinary experience is not merely a collection of private outcomes. Scientific facts are shared. Measurement records persist. Different observers can inspect the same apparatus, read the same notebook, and agree about the same result. A theory of frame-relative actualization must therefore explain how locally bound outcomes become stable public records.

Frames Theory answers by distinguishing local definiteness from classical objectivity. An outcome becomes definite for a frame when that frame binds to an outcome-relative state. An outcome becomes objective for a network when the same outcome-relative information is redundantly stabilized across many compatible frames.

The central thesis of this paper is:

> Classical objectivity is redundant frame-binding across a recursive information network.

This thesis does not add new dynamics to quantum mechanics. It reinterprets the standard decoherence story in frame-theoretic terms. Decoherence explains why interference between some alternatives becomes inaccessible. Quantum Darwinism explains how environmental redundancy allows many observers to access the same records. Frames Theory incorporates both insights but adds a broader ontology: records are not merely environmental fragments; they are stabilized bindings across a nested and lateral network of frames.

The result is a bridge between quantum frame-binding and classical facthood. A classical fact is not a primitive ontological category. It is a high-stability, high-redundancy, high-accessibility binding structure.

## 2. From Quantum Binding to Classical Records

Frames Theory treats measurement as a staged process rather than a single metaphysical jump. A quantum system begins in an outcome context such as

$$
|\psi\rangle_S=\sum_k c_k|k\rangle_S.
$$

A receiving frame $F$ initially in state $|F_0\rangle$ can become correlated with the system through an interaction of the form

$$
|\psi\rangle_S|F_0\rangle
\longrightarrow
\sum_k c_k|k\rangle_S|F_k\rangle.
$$

This is the entangling stage of quantum frame-binding. The system and frame are no longer described as independent factors. Each possible system state is correlated with a corresponding frame state.

Entangling correlation alone, however, is not enough to produce a classical record. Entanglement may be microscopic, reversible, and inaccessible to most surrounding frames. A classical record requires more than correlation. It requires stabilization, persistence, and accessibility.

A more complete measurement chain includes environmental propagation:

$$
\sum_k c_k|k\rangle_S|F_k\rangle|E_0\rangle
\longrightarrow
\sum_k c_k|k\rangle_S|F_k\rangle|E_k\rangle.
$$

Here $E$ represents environmental degrees of freedom that become correlated with the outcome-relative frame states. If the environmental states become effectively orthogonal,

$$
\langle E_j|E_k\rangle\approx 0
\quad\text{for}\quad j\ne k,
$$

then interference between the alternatives becomes inaccessible to frames that interact only with coarse environmental records. The effective state available to such frames is approximately diagonal:

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}
\approx
\sum_k |c_k|^2 |k\rangle\langle k|.
$$

In Frames Theory, this is not universal collapse. It is the emergence of a stable frame-relative record. Decoherence does not make one outcome absolutely real for all possible descriptions. It makes certain alternatives inaccessible and certain records robust for the frames coupled to that environment.

The transition from quantum binding to classical record therefore proceeds through a layered sequence:

1. **Entangling correlation:** the system and receiving frame become non-factorizably correlated.
2. **Decoherent stabilization:** environmental propagation suppresses accessible interference between alternatives.
3. **Informational accessibility:** compatible frames can access the stabilized record.
4. **Redundant frame-binding:** many frames stabilize the same outcome-relative information.
5. **Classical objectivity:** the record becomes public, durable, and mutually confirmable within a frame network.

The crucial point is that classical objectivity is not the negation of frame-relativity. It is what frame-relativity becomes when many compatible frames converge on the same stabilized information.

## 3. Defining Redundant Frame-Binding

A single receiving frame may bind to an outcome-relative state. This gives local definiteness. But a classical fact requires more. It requires that the outcome-relative information be stabilized across multiple frames.

Call this **redundant frame-binding**.

A binding is redundant when the same outcome-relative information is accessible through many distinct but compatible frame pathways. A detector may encode the result in its internal state. An apparatus may encode it as a pointer position. The surrounding air, photons, thermal degrees of freedom, and electromagnetic environment may carry partial records. An observer may encode it in memory. A notebook or database may encode it in a durable external medium.

These are not identical records in a metaphysical sense. They are distinct physical frames whose states map onto the same outcome class. The stability of the classical fact comes from this redundancy.

A schematic containment chain may look like this:

$$
S \leftrightarrow F_{\mathrm{detector}}
\leftrightarrow F_{\mathrm{apparatus}}
\leftrightarrow F_{\mathrm{lab}}
\leftrightarrow F_{\mathrm{observer}}
\leftrightarrow F_{\mathrm{record}}.
$$

This chain should not be read as a simple linear pipeline. In real systems, propagation is distributed and network-like. Information spreads upward into containing frames and laterally into neighboring environmental frames. The important point is that compatible frames become able to recover the same outcome-relative distinction.

A fact is objective to the extent that it satisfies three conditions.

First, it is **stable**. The record persists under ordinary interactions and is not immediately erased by microscopic reversibility.

Second, it is **accessible**. Multiple frames can obtain the relevant information without requiring access to the full microscopic state.

Third, it is **redundant**. The same outcome-relative information is encoded across many frame states, so no single fragile correlation bears the entire burden of facthood.

In this sense, objectivity is not all-or-nothing. It comes in degrees. A microscopic entanglement with no stable record has low objectivity. A detector click recorded in an apparatus and visible in environmental photons has higher objectivity. A published measurement result preserved in many records and independently checked by many laboratories has still higher objectivity.

## 4. Decoherence, Quantum Darwinism, and Frames Theory

The idea that objectivity emerges through redundant environmental records is closely related to quantum Darwinism. Quantum Darwinism argues that classical objectivity arises because environments do not merely decohere systems; they also store many redundant copies or fragments of information about pointer states. Observers can independently sample different environmental fragments and infer the same result.

Frames Theory is compatible with this view. It accepts that environmental redundancy is central to classical objectivity. But it generalizes the account in several ways.

First, Frames Theory treats environmental fragments as frames or parts of frames. The environment is not a passive background; it is a distributed network of information-bearing systems.

Second, Frames Theory distinguishes propagation, binding, actualization, and accessibility. A signal may propagate without binding. A frame may be exposed without being compatible. A binding may occur without becoming widely objective. Classical objectivity requires the stronger condition of redundant, accessible stabilization.

Third, Frames Theory embeds environmental redundancy inside recursive containment. A detector is contained in an apparatus, the apparatus in a laboratory, the laboratory in an environment, and the environment in broader physical contexts. Objectivity therefore involves not only lateral redundancy across environmental fragments, but also vertical reconciliation across nested frames.

Fourth, Frames Theory connects redundancy to the Born-rule interpretation. The Born rule gives the measure of possible frame-bindings. Classical objectivity describes what happens after one outcome-relative binding becomes stabilized and redundantly propagated through a frame network.

A compact way to state the relation is:

$$
\text{classical objectivity}
\approx
\text{decoherence}
+
\text{redundant accessibility}
+
\text{frame consistency}.
$$

This expression is not meant as a new equation of motion. It is a conceptual decomposition. Decoherence suppresses accessible interference. Redundant accessibility allows multiple frames to recover the same record. Frame consistency ensures that these records reconcile across nested and overlapping descriptions.

## 5. Classical Facts Without Universal Collapse

The emergence of classical objectivity often tempts one to reintroduce collapse. If many observers agree on one result, it is natural to say that the world itself must have selected that result absolutely. Frames Theory resists that move.

A classical fact is not a universal collapse event. It is a stabilized structure of agreement across frames. The reason the outcome seems absolute is that the relevant frame network has become saturated with redundant records of it. For all practical frames in that network, the outcome is fixed, accessible, and repeatable.

This explains why classical facts are robust without requiring metaphysical absoluteness. A broader or differently situated frame may still describe the total system-environment state unitarily. But ordinary frames embedded in the decohered record network cannot access the phase relations required to reverse or interfere the alternatives. For them, the outcome is a classical fact.

Thus Frames Theory distinguishes three statuses.

**Frame-relative actualization.** An outcome is definite for a receiving frame that has bound to it.

**Redundant objectivity.** An outcome is stabilized across many compatible frames and can be independently accessed through multiple records.

**Universal absoluteness.** An outcome is selected as the single state of reality for all possible frames.

Frames Theory accepts the first two and rejects the need for the third. Classical reality is built from frame-relative actualizations that have become redundantly stabilized, not from a universal collapse imposed from outside the frame network.

This also explains why classical facts are not merely subjective. The objectivity of a record does not come from private belief. It comes from physical redundancy. A notebook entry, detector state, memory trace, photon field, and database record are all physical frames or frame states. Their agreement is an objective relational structure, even though it remains frame-indexed.

## 6. Nested Frames and Record Propagation

Classical records do not merely spread sideways into an environment. They also move through nested containment structures.

A detector may first bind to a microscopic event. The apparatus then binds to the detector state. The laboratory environment binds to the apparatus state through photons, air molecules, thermal exchange, and mechanical stability. An observer binds to the laboratory state through sensory interaction. A notebook, file, or database binds to the observer-apparatus interaction through inscription or storage. Later observers bind to the record.

The same outcome-relative information therefore propagates through a nested sequence:

$$
F_{\mathrm{detector}}
\subseteq
F_{\mathrm{apparatus}}
\subseteq
F_{\mathrm{lab}}
\subseteq
F_{\mathrm{community}}.
$$

The containment symbol here should not be understood as a strict spatial box relation. It represents informational embedding. A frame can be contained in another when its records, constraints, and state transitions are accessible within the broader frame's informational domain.

For objectivity to emerge, these levels must reconcile. A fine-grained detector frame may distinguish microchannels that the apparatus frame does not. The apparatus may register only a coarse pointer state. The observer may register only a displayed value. The scientific community may register only a published result. Yet these levels can still describe the same event if their outcome classes map consistently.

A consistency relation can be written as

$$
\mu_{F_P}(k)=\sum_{\alpha\in\pi^{-1}(k)}\mu_{F_i}(\alpha),
$$

where $F_i$ is a finer frame, $F_P$ is a parent or containing frame, $\alpha$ are fine-grained distinctions, and $\pi$ maps those distinctions to the coarse outcome $k$.

In the present paper, this relation is not used to derive the Born rule. It is used to clarify how classical facts remain stable across levels of description. A coarse fact is objective when it is supported by a consistent family of finer bindings beneath it and redundant records around it.

## 7. Degrees of Objectivity

Frames Theory suggests that objectivity should be treated as graded rather than binary.

At the lowest level are fleeting entangling correlations. These may exist only briefly, may be reversible, and may not be accessible to any stable receiving frame. They have little or no classical objectivity.

At the next level are local bindings. A receiving frame has stabilized an outcome-relative state, but the information has not yet propagated widely. The outcome is definite for that frame, but not yet public.

At the next level are decoherent bindings. Environmental propagation has made interference inaccessible and has stabilized the record in a pointer-like basis. The outcome is robust for a class of frames coupled to that environment.

At the next level are redundant records. Many frames independently encode the same outcome-relative information. The outcome becomes public, durable, and practically objective.

At the highest ordinary level are institutional or scientific records. Multiple laboratories, observers, documents, instruments, and theoretical frameworks align around the same coarse-grained result. Such objectivity is not metaphysical absoluteness. It is highly redundant, cross-frame consistency.

This graded view matches ordinary scientific practice. A single unrecorded perception is weak evidence. A detector click is stronger. A logged instrument reading is stronger still. A reproducible experiment across laboratories is stronger again. Frames Theory interprets this progression as increasing redundancy and consistency across frames.

## 8. Relation to the Prior Papers

This paper depends on the prior four papers but adds a distinct step.

The first paper, *Frames Theory: A Relational Information Framework for Physical Reality*, provides the ontology of frames, observation, containment, and consistency.

The second paper, *The Born Rule as a Frame-Binding Measure in Frames Theory*, explains what Born probability measures: possible successful bindings between quantum phenomena and receiving frames.

The third paper, *Frame-Relative State Update*, explains how projection, partial trace, and decoherence function as frame-relative update operations.

The fourth paper, *Toward a Frames-Theoretic Derivation of the Born Rule*, asks why the binding measure must be quadratic.

The present paper asks a different question: how do frame-relative outcomes become classical public facts?

Its answer is that public facthood emerges from redundant frame-binding. This makes the sequence coherent:

$$
\text{frames}
\longrightarrow
\text{binding measure}
\longrightarrow
\text{state update}
\longrightarrow
\text{measure derivation}
\longrightarrow
\text{classical objectivity}.
$$

One could also view this paper as completing the measurement arc. The earlier papers explain how outcomes become definite for frames. This paper explains how those outcomes become stable across networks of frames.

## 9. Relation to Existing Approaches

### 9.1 Decoherence Theory

Frames Theory agrees with decoherence theory that environmental interactions suppress accessible interference and stabilize pointer-like states. It does not treat decoherence as collapse. Decoherence is the process by which some correlations become inaccessible and others become stable for particular frames.

The contribution of Frames Theory is to interpret decoherence as part of a broader process of frame-binding and record propagation.

### 9.2 Quantum Darwinism

Quantum Darwinism explains objectivity through redundant environmental records. Frames Theory is strongly aligned with this idea. However, Frames Theory broadens the account beyond environmental fragments alone. It includes detector frames, apparatus frames, observer frames, documentary frames, and institutional frames, all connected through accessibility and consistency relations.

Quantum Darwinism explains why many observers can sample the environment and agree. Frames Theory adds a general language for how frames bind, stabilize, propagate, and reconcile information.

### 9.3 Everettian Interpretations

Everettian interpretations explain classicality through branching and decoherence. Frames Theory shares the no-universal-collapse structure but avoids making branches the primary ontology. The primary units are frames and their binding relations.

On this view, objectivity is not a branch becoming real. It is a record becoming redundantly accessible across a frame network.

### 9.4 Relational Quantum Mechanics

Relational Quantum Mechanics treats properties as relative to interacting systems. Frames Theory agrees that facts are relational, but it adds the machinery of redundancy and containment. A fact is not merely relative to one interaction; it can become stable across many interactions and many frames.

This allows Frames Theory to explain why classical facts feel less fragile than isolated relational events.

### 9.5 QBism

QBism emphasizes agent-centered probability and experience. Frames Theory is not agent-centered in this way. Redundant frame-binding is a physical process involving detectors, environments, records, and observers. Agreement among agents is a special case of broader agreement among physical frames.

## 10. Limitations and Open Problems

This paper is interpretive and structural. It does not alter the predictions of decoherence theory or quantum Darwinism. It does not propose a new dynamical collapse mechanism.

It also does not provide a full quantitative theory of redundancy. A future technical treatment should define a measure of frame redundancy, perhaps in terms of mutual information, environmental fragments, or consistency functionals over frame networks.

It does not fully solve the preferred-basis problem. It argues that pointer bases are frame-relative stabilization bases determined by coupling and accessibility. A deeper theory would need to specify how those bases emerge in general settings.

It does not yet address relativistic constraints on record propagation. In relativistic settings, accessibility is constrained by causal structure, light cones, and horizons. That should be treated in a later paper.

It does not address cosmological objectivity, where no external observer frame can encompass the whole universe. That too belongs to a later stage of the research program.

Finally, this paper assumes that frames can be treated as relatively stable informational contexts. A deeper treatment would need to explain how such frames emerge and persist physically.

## 11. Conclusion

Frames Theory begins with frame-relative actualization: outcomes become definite for receiving frames. But classical reality requires more than local definiteness. It requires stable records, shared accessibility, and cross-frame agreement.

This paper has argued that classical objectivity emerges through redundant frame-binding. A quantum event begins as entangling correlation, becomes stabilized through decoherence, becomes accessible through environmental and record-bearing propagation, and becomes objective when many compatible frames can recover the same outcome-relative information.

The core formula is conceptual rather than dynamical:

$$
\text{classical objectivity}
\approx
\text{decoherent stabilization}
+
\text{redundant accessibility}
+
\text{frame consistency}.
$$

This preserves local definiteness without universal collapse. It also explains why classical facts are not merely private experiences. A classical fact is a stable, redundant, consistency-preserving structure in a network of physical frames.

In this sense, classical reality is not a separate ontological layer imposed on quantum mechanics. It is the high-redundancy limit of frame-relative binding.
