# General Relativity in Frames Theory

William J. House

## Abstract

General Relativity is usually interpreted as a theory in which mass-energy curves spacetime and free bodies follow geodesics within that curved geometry. Frames Theory does not reject this mathematics, but it offers a different ontological reading. Instead of treating spacetime curvature as the deformation of a pre-existing substance, Frames Theory interprets General Relativity as the rule-set governing how local measurement frames remain mutually consistent when energy, momentum, clocks, rods, and causal signals are distributed non-uniformly.

The central claim of this paper is that General Relativity can be read as a classical theory of frame compatibility. Local inertial frames remain primary. The metric becomes the local measurement-binding rule that translates coordinate differences into physically meaningful intervals. Curvature becomes the non-closure of frame comparisons around extended paths. Geodesics become consistency-preserving histories through a non-flat frame neighborhood. Horizons become causal frame-connectivity boundaries. Singularities are reinterpreted not as physical objects, but as breakdowns in the classical frame-binding model.

This paper does not attempt to replace General Relativity, nor does it derive Einstein's field equations from Frames Theory. Its goal is interpretive and architectural. It argues that GR is the classical, continuous, differentiable limit of frame-binding consistency and that its geometry is best understood as the bookkeeping structure of admissible relations among local frames. This reinterpretation prepares a bridge between relativistic geometry, quantum measurement, cosmology, and the broader Frames Theory research program.

## 1. Introduction

Frames Theory begins not with detached objects moving in a passive container, but with bounded physical informational frames. A frame is a locally coherent measurement context: a domain within which clocks, rulers, signals, records, and constraints can be defined and reconciled. Observation is directed information transfer between frames. Measurement is a stabilized binding between a receiving frame and outcome-relative information. Classical objectivity emerges when many frames redundantly bind to the same record structure.

General Relativity already privileges local frames. Its most basic lesson is that no single inertial frame covers the whole universe. Instead, each observer has a local inertial frame in which physics appears approximately special-relativistic, while large-scale comparison among many such frames reveals curvature, redshift, horizon structure, and gravitational time dilation. This makes GR unusually compatible with a Frames Theory reinterpretation.

The aim of this paper is not to claim that Einstein's theory is wrong. The aim is to restate what GR is doing in a more explicitly frame-centered ontology. The guiding question is:

> What if General Relativity is best understood not as a theory of a bent spacetime substance, but as a theory of global consistency conditions among local observer frames?

The central thesis of this paper is:

> In Frames Theory, General Relativity is the large-scale compatibility theory of local measurement frames in the presence of non-uniform energy and momentum.

This reformulation preserves the mathematical content of GR while shifting the ontological emphasis. The metric is reinterpreted as a local frame-binding rule. Curvature becomes path-dependent mismatch among frame translations. Geodesics become consistency-preserving histories. Horizons become causal information boundaries within a frame network. The result is a picture in which geometry is not abandoned, but understood as the emergent bookkeeping structure of admissible relations among frames.

The paper proceeds in three stages. First, it restates the core move of General Relativity in frame-centered language. Second, it develops a concept-by-concept mapping from GR vocabulary to Frames Theory vocabulary. Third, it argues that this reinterpretation clarifies why GR should be treated as the classical smooth limit of a broader frame-binding ontology rather than as a rival to it.

This paper also occupies a specific place in the broader sequence. The earlier relativistic paper developed causal accessibility, horizons, and bounded information domains without yet giving a dedicated reinterpretation of gravitation itself. The Big Bang paper later treated cosmology as a boundary and inheritance problem. The present paper sits between those two tasks. It interprets gravity, curvature, geodesics, and relativistic measurement consistency in a way that makes the later cosmological extension more natural.

## 2. From Newtonian Gravity to Relativistic Frame Consistency

In Newtonian mechanics, gravity is a force acting between masses within a common background space and time. Space and time are treated as already given. The gravitational interaction is then an additional dynamical ingredient placed inside that universal stage.

General Relativity changes the problem fundamentally. In GR, gravity is not primarily an external force law but a geometric relation between energy-momentum content and the local structure of measurement. Einstein's field equation is written as:

$$
G_{\mu\nu}=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}
$$

Very roughly, this says:

$$
\text{spacetime curvature}=\text{energy-momentum content}.
$$

Objects in free fall follow geodesics, that is, locally straight histories in a curved spacetime geometry. The standard interpretation says that matter tells spacetime how to curve, and curved spacetime tells matter how to move.

Frames Theory proposes a parallel restatement:

> A gravitating system is a network of local frames whose measurements cannot all be globally flattened into one universal frame without distortion.

That distortion is what General Relativity calls curvature. This does not alter the predictive content of GR. It reframes the ontology from curved substance to constrained frame compatibility.

The next step is therefore to identify the minimal conceptual dictionary required for that reframing. What exactly corresponds, in Frames Theory language, to local inertial frames, to curvature, to the metric, and to free motion? The following section gives a compact formal sketch before the paper returns to conceptual exposition.

## 3. A Minimal Formal Translation

The interpretive move of this paper can be summarized in a compact formal sketch. Let a local frame be represented as

$$
F_i=(M_i,R_i,C_i),
$$

where $M_i$ is the local measurement basis, $R_i$ is the set of admissible relations that can be operationally resolved within the frame, and $C_i$ is the set of consistency constraints governing those relations.

In flat relativistic settings, frame translation between neighboring local frames can be represented schematically as a transformation map

$$
T_{ij}:F_i \to F_j
$$

such that extended compositions close consistently across the neighborhood. In curved settings, the composition of these local translation maps becomes path dependent. For a closed path $\gamma$ through a frame neighborhood,

$$
T_{\gamma} \neq I
$$

in the generic case, where $I$ denotes trivial closure. This failure of closure is what GR describes geometrically as curvature and what Frames Theory interprets relationally as nontrivial mismatch among local frame translations.

The metric tensor then appears as the local rule that binds coordinate differences to measured interval structure,

$$
ds^2=g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu,
$$

while geodesics are the histories that preserve local consistency without requiring additional frame-correction terms. This paper does not claim that these schematic translations constitute a derivation of GR. Their purpose is narrower: to show that the mathematical objects of General Relativity already admit a natural reinterpretation as structures governing the compatibility of bounded measurement frames.

With this scaffold in place, the remaining sections can proceed concept by concept. The aim is not to mathematize every step again, but to show that the standard interpretive vocabulary of GR already maps cleanly onto a frame-centered ontology.

## 4. Local Frames and Global Compatibility

The first decisive move in a Frames Theory reading of GR is to preserve the primacy of local frames. In a sufficiently small neighborhood, each observer has a locally coherent measurement basis. The local metric may be approximated by the Minkowski form,

$$
ds^2=-c^2 d\tau^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2.
$$

In ordinary GR language, this means that spacetime is locally flat. In Frames Theory language, it means that each local frame has a coherent internal measurement rule: clocks, rulers, causal ordering, and signal propagation are locally reconcilable.

The problem emerges only when these local descriptions are stitched together over extended regions. In the presence of mass-energy, the local rules of many neighboring frames do not combine into one globally flat structure. Large-scale comparison introduces mismatch, non-closure, and path dependence.

This suggests a clean reinterpretation:

> General Relativity is the global compatibility theory of local frames.

The key object of study is not an absolute external geometry independently given before measurement, but the consistency conditions under which many local frames can coexist, compare measurements, and preserve causal order in a non-uniform physical environment. The earlier relativistic Frames Theory paper made the analogous point for light cones and causal accessibility: no frame has unconstrained access to all records. The present paper extends that move by claiming that even when local records are coherent, the large-scale comparison among many such frames can fail to close into one globally flat structure.

## 5. Curvature as Non-Closure of Frame Relations

Curvature is often introduced mathematically through the Riemann tensor or physically through tidal effects and parallel transport. Frames Theory translates this into a relational statement:

> Curvature measures the failure of frame comparisons to close consistently around extended paths.

If one transports a vector around a closed loop on a curved surface, it may return with a changed orientation. Likewise, in spacetime, clocks, rods, inertial directions, and light-cone structures need not return to their original mutual alignment after extended comparison around a gravitating region.

This gives a Frames Theory interpretation of curvature:

- flat spacetime: frame translations close globally;
- curved spacetime: frame translations close only locally and fail globally;
- tidal structure: gradients in the mismatch among neighboring frames.

Gravity is therefore not an extra invisible substance or force laid atop otherwise independent frames. It is the observable consequence of nontrivial frame-binding relations across an extended neighborhood.

## 6. The Metric as a Measurement-Binding Rule

In General Relativity, the metric tensor determines how coordinate differences are converted into invariant intervals:

$$
ds^2=g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu.
$$

Frames Theory interprets the metric as more than a formal field. It becomes:

> The local rule for binding coordinate bookkeeping to physically meaningful measured intervals.

Coordinates by themselves are not physical. They are labels. What is physical are the invariant relations: proper time, causal separation, spatial interval, redshift structure, and admissible signal paths. The metric tells a local frame how to interpret coordinate differences as measured relations.

In this sense, the metric is the local measurement-binding rule of a frame neighborhood. It governs:

- comparison of clocks,
- comparison of rulers,
- distinction among timelike, spacelike, and null separations,
- stitching of neighboring measurement frames,
- preservation of local causal consistency.

This reinterpretation preserves the mathematics while clarifying the ontology. The metric is not merely a field on a background; it is the rule by which neighboring frames become mutually intelligible.

Once the metric is read in this way, the equivalence principle becomes easier to reinterpret. The question is no longer whether gravity is “really” a force or “really” geometry in a substance-like container. The question is how a local frame experiences deformation when embedded in a larger field of frame relations.

## 7. The Equivalence Principle Recast

Einstein's equivalence principle states that locally, a uniform gravitational field is indistinguishable from acceleration. Frames Theory can restate this directly:

> A local frame cannot determine, using only local measurements, whether its measurement deformation arises from acceleration through a frame neighborhood or from being embedded in a gravitational frame gradient.

This provides a natural bridge between inertial deviation and gravitational structure. The distinction between acceleration and gravity becomes visible only across extended comparison among separated frames. Gravity exhibits tidal structure because it reflects a nontrivial pattern of mismatch across a broader neighborhood. Pure acceleration, by contrast, need not generate the same extended incompatibility pattern.

The conceptual translation is:

| GR Concept | Frames Theory Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Local inertial frame | Locally coherent observer-measurement frame |
| Acceleration | Change in frame relation relative to nearby frames |
| Gravity | Extended non-closure of frame relations |
| Tidal effect | Gradient in frame mismatch across a neighborhood |
| Curvature | Global incompatibility of flattening all local frames |

This translation also prevents a common misunderstanding. Frames Theory is not reducing gravity to a mere artifact of perspective. The claim is not that gravity is unreal, but that what is physically real is the structured incompatibility among frame relations, and that GR already provides the mathematical language for that incompatibility.

## 8. Geodesics and Consistency-Preserving Histories

In GR, freely falling bodies follow geodesics. Frames Theory interprets this as:

> A geodesic is the history through a frame neighborhood that requires no additional frame-correction.

Free-fall is therefore not best understood as an object being pulled by an external force. It is an object following the locally consistent path determined by the surrounding frame-binding structure. A body appears weightless in orbit because it follows such a path even though its motion is globally curved relative to Earth-centered coordinates.

Conversely, what is ordinarily felt as weight may be reinterpreted as the local correction imposed when a body is prevented from following its natural consistency-preserving history. Standing on the Earth's surface is non-inertial precisely because the surface blocks the geodesic one would otherwise follow.

This interpretation becomes especially important in strong-field settings, where frame accessibility itself is modified. Once geodesic motion and free-fall are understood in this way, horizons and singular structures can be re-examined as problems of frame-connectivity and model breakdown rather than as curiosities of coordinate description.

## 9. Horizons, Singularities, and Frame Boundaries

Strong-field GR introduces horizons and singularities. Frames Theory suggests that these should be treated primarily as frame-connectivity problems.

A black hole horizon is not a material membrane. It is a causal boundary. In Frames Theory terms:

> A horizon is a boundary in the frame neighborhood where certain outward frame-communications cease to be physically admissible.

Frames outside the horizon can still exchange signals with the broader universe. Frames inside cannot send future-directed signals outward. The horizon is therefore a connectivity boundary in the causal frame network.

The singularity should be treated more cautiously. Frames Theory need not elevate it to a literal physical object. A more disciplined reading is:

> A singularity marks the breakdown of the classical GR frame-binding model.

Where the known rules no longer define a coherent measurement neighborhood, the ontology should not simply reify the formal divergence. This approach aligns well with the broader Frames Theory tendency to interpret singular structures as limits or failures of a model rather than as unquestioned physical primitives.

## 10. Cosmology as Evolving Inter-Frame Binding

Standard relativistic cosmology often describes expansion through a scale factor in an FLRW-like metric. Frames Theory translates this into a relational form:

> Cosmic expansion is not primarily motion through a pre-existing container, but a changing large-scale rule for inter-frame distance and comparison among comoving frames.

Galaxies are not simply fragments flying through a static arena. The large-scale relation among frames evolves. The binding rule that determines how distances, causal reach, and signal timing compare across cosmic neighborhoods changes over time.

This reinforces the general Frames Theory theme: physical content lies in evolving relations among frames, not in an independently given container that exists first and is populated afterward.

At this point the relation to the later cosmological papers becomes explicit. The earlier relativistic paper introduced causal domains and horizon-bounded access. The Big Bang paper treated cosmology as a boundary and inheritance problem. The present paper supplies the missing middle layer: the classical geometric compatibility structure that governs how local relativistic frames coexist before one asks how entire cosmological frame domains emerge, evolve, or terminate.

## 11. What Frames Theory Clarifies in General Relativity

General Relativity already possesses extraordinary mathematical power. The value of a Frames Theory interpretation is not to replace that power, but to clarify several persistent interpretive tensions:

- Why do coordinates have no direct physical meaning?
- Why does gravity disappear locally but return globally?
- What is meant by curvature being physically real?
- Why should horizons be understood causally rather than materially?
- What exactly is breaking down at a singularity?

Frames Theory answers these by treating geometry as the emergent bookkeeping structure of admissible frame relations. Coordinates are labels. The metric is a measurement-binding rule. Gravity disappears locally because local frame coherence remains intact. Gravity reappears globally because many local frame relations fail to close into one flat structure. Horizons are causal accessibility boundaries. Singularities are failures of the classical frame-binding description.

## 12. General Relativity as the Classical Limit of Frames Theory

The most important strategic role of this reinterpretation is not internal to GR alone. It is what it does for the broader theory program.

Frames Theory should not say that General Relativity is wrong. It should say:

> General Relativity is the classical, continuous, differentiable limit of frame-binding consistency when the relevant measurement frames are macroscopic and quantum discreteness can be ignored.

This gives a natural ladder of interpretation:

| Domain | Frames Theory View |
| --- | --- |
| Special Relativity | Flat frame transformations |
| General Relativity | Curved frame-neighborhood consistency |
| Quantum Mechanics | Probabilistic frame binding at measurement |
| Entanglement | Nonclassical frame correlation |
| Cosmology | Evolution of the global frame neighborhood |
| Singularities | Boundaries or failures of classical frame ontology |

Under this reading, GR and QM are not competing total ontologies. They are different regimes of frame relation. GR treats smooth classical frame compatibility. QM treats discrete, probabilistic, measurement-dependent frame binding. A future unification would therefore aim not at stitching together two alien theories, but at recovering both as special cases of one deeper frame-binding structure.

This is why the present paper matters within the sequence. The earlier paper on relativistic frames established causal boundedness. The later cosmological paper treated the universe as a bounded or inherited frame domain. The present paper explains what the classical geometric content of that intermediate regime looks like when described as frame compatibility rather than spacetime substance.

## 13. Limitations and Open Problems

This paper is interpretive and architectural rather than derivational. It does not derive Einstein's field equations from Frames Theory, and it does not claim that the differential-geometric formalism of GR can simply be discarded and replaced by a purely verbal notion of frame compatibility. Its aim is to clarify ontology, not to supersede mathematics.

Several limitations should be stated explicitly.

First, the formal translation developed here is schematic. The notation of frame-translation maps and non-closure conditions is meant to clarify interpretation, not to provide a complete alternative formalism to pseudo-Riemannian geometry, affine connection, curvature tensors, or geodesic equations.

Second, this paper does not yet show how the full local differential structure of GR should be reconstructed from a deeper frame-binding theory. If Frames Theory is to become more than an interpretive lens, it will eventually need a stronger account of how metric structure, curvature, and causal structure emerge from more primitive frame relations.

Third, the treatment of singularities as breakdowns of the classical frame-binding model is conceptually attractive, but still programmatic. A future theory would need to show what replaces the classical description near such boundaries and how that replacement connects to quantum gravity, cosmology, or horizon thermodynamics.

Fourth, the relation between this GR reinterpretation and the quantum side of Frames Theory remains incomplete. The present paper argues that GR is the smooth classical limit of frame-binding consistency, but it does not yet provide the dynamical bridge by which quantum measurement structure and relativistic geometry are recovered from one common formal basis.

These are not defects in the immediate argument. They define the next layer of work. The present paper establishes that General Relativity can be re-read coherently within Frames Theory. Later work must determine whether that reinterpretation can be deepened into a fuller constructive account.

## 14. Conclusion

General Relativity can be read in Frames Theory without rewriting its mathematics. The change is interpretive rather than predictive. Instead of treating gravity as the bending of a spacetime substance, Frames Theory interprets GR as the theory of large-scale compatibility conditions among local observer frames.

The metric is the local measurement-binding rule. Curvature is the nontrivial mismatch among frame translations across extended paths. Geodesics are consistency-preserving histories. Horizons are frame-connectivity boundaries. Singularities signal the breakdown of the classical frame-binding model.

The resulting formulation is concise:

> General Relativity is Frames Theory applied to spacetime measurement consistency.

This reading matters because it preserves the success of GR while integrating it into a larger ontology in which measurement, causality, geometry, and probability are understood as special cases of frame-binding constraints.

In the context of the broader series, this paper should be read together with the earlier treatment of relativistic causal information boundaries and the later treatment of cosmological boundary structure. Those papers establish, respectively, the bounded accessibility of relativistic frames and the admissibility-boundary interpretation of cosmology. The present paper supplies the classical geometric bridge between them.
