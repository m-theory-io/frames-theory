# A Frames-Theoretic Big Bang

William J. House

## Abstract

The Big Bang is often described as the beginning of space, time, matter, and physical law. In classical general relativity, extrapolating the expansion of the universe backward leads toward a singularity. But a singularity is not a physical object, event, or ordinary boundary. It is a sign that the classical description has broken down. This paper develops a Frames Theory alternative: the Big Bang should be modeled not as an absolute point-origin, but as a cosmological frame-boundary surface or transition interface between aeonic, quantum-geometric, or otherwise pre-classical domains and the observable post-boundary universe.

The central proposal is that the Big Bang is a cosmological admissibility boundary. It determines which aspects can become time-bearing, record-bearing, field-bearing, and geometry-bearing inside the present universe. In a Penrose-compatible aeonic reading, smoothing need not be a post-boundary inflationary process. If a previous aeon approaches heat death, mass-bearing particles decay, black holes evaporate, ordinary records dilute, and scale-dependent structure loses operational meaning. The boundary frame may therefore inherit an already smoothed, conformally admissible condition. Frames Theory treats inflation not as a required primitive, but as one possible model to be compared against a boundary-conditioned observable account. The task is not to assume an inflaton, but to explain cosmological observables through boundary inheritance, constraint selection, field-frame differentiation, and post-boundary record growth.

## 1. Introduction

Frames Theory begins with bounded informational frames rather than detached objects viewed from nowhere. A frame is a physical informational context with an internal state, accessible relations, constraints, and possible records. Observation is directed information transfer between frames. Measurement is a special case in which a receiving frame becomes stably bound to outcome-relative information.

Earlier papers developed Frames Theory in stages: basic frame ontology, Born weights as frame-binding measures, frame-relative state update, decoherence, classical objectivity, entanglement, causal boundaries, cosmological horizons, records, aspect-selective binding, Hilbert space as frame-neighborhood state space, and quantum fields as frames. The present paper asks what the Big Bang becomes in this framework.

The guiding question is:

> Is the Big Bang an absolute origin, a singular boundary, a bounce, a conformal transition, or a frame-boundary transformation?

Frames Theory does not need to decide in advance among all cosmological models. It can instead ask a more general question:

> What, if anything, can bind across the Big Bang boundary?

This paper proposes that the Big Bang should be treated as a cosmological frame boundary: an extreme transition surface where prior or pre-classical aspects may be erased, transformed, filtered, or inherited as constraints in the current universe.

The central thesis is:

> In Frames Theory, the Big Bang is not merely an initial time. It is a cosmological admissibility boundary that determines which aspects can become time-bearing, record-bearing, field-bearing, and geometry-bearing inside the observable universe.

This paper is deliberately cautious. It does not prove conformal cyclic cosmology, loop quantum cosmology, a bounce, or any specific pre-Big-Bang model. It does not derive the observed universe from first principles. It develops a vocabulary for thinking about the Big Bang as a boundary on frame accessibility and inheritance, rather than as a singular point at which explanation stops.

## 2. The Big Bang as a Boundary Problem

In standard cosmology, the observable universe was once hot, dense, and rapidly evolving. The cosmic microwave background, light-element abundances, large-scale structure, and expansion history provide strong evidence for an early hot phase. The phrase "Big Bang" can refer to this early hot dense state, or more strongly to a mathematical singularity reached by extrapolating classical general relativity backward.

Frames Theory should distinguish these two meanings.

The hot early universe is a physical regime with records, observables, and modelable processes. The singularity is different. It is not an observed event. It is a breakdown of a classical description. Treating the singularity as a literal physical point risks mistaking a failure of representation for a piece of ontology.

Frames Theory therefore begins by discarding the singularity as a physical primitive. Once the singularity is discarded, the Big Bang can be modeled as a boundary or transition surface. That surface need not be a spatial surface inside ordinary spacetime. It may be a conformal boundary, a quantum-geometric transition, an aeonic interface, or a limiting surface of frame accessibility.

This move changes the question. Instead of asking what happened at a point of infinite density, Frames Theory asks:

- What frame-relative description is valid near the earliest accessible universe?
- What structures fail at the boundary?
- Which aspects are erased or transformed?
- Which constraints can be inherited into the post-boundary universe?
- What observable records could such inheritance produce?

The Big Bang is then no longer the place where inquiry ends. It becomes a boundary condition on what can become physically meaningful in the current cosmic frame.

## 3. Cosmological Frames and Accessibility

A cosmological frame is a bounded domain of physical description at cosmic scale. The observable universe is one such frame: it contains the records, signals, fields, horizons, and causal relations accessible to observers within it.

The observable universe is not the same as all possible reality. It is the post-boundary domain available to us through causal and record-bearing channels. In Frames Theory language:

$$
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{obs}}=\text{post-boundary cosmological frame accessible through records}.
$$

The frame includes the cosmic microwave background, light-element abundances, large-scale structure, gravitational records, particle-physics records, and astronomical observations. These records do not give direct access to a pre-boundary domain. They give access to post-boundary traces, constraints, and reconstructions.

This distinction matters. If there was a previous aeon, a bounce, or a pre-geometric phase, no observer inside the current universe directly observes it. Any evidence must appear as accessible records or constraints in the current frame.

Thus:

$$
\text{pre-boundary event}
\notin
\text{direct observational access},
$$

but

$$
\text{post-boundary constraint or record}
\in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{obs}}.
$$

Frames Theory treats all cross-boundary claims as inferential. They must be reconciled across independent record frames: cosmology, particle physics, quantum gravity, entropy, and large-scale structure.

## 4. The Big Bang as Frame-Boundary Surface

Once the singularity is removed from the ontology, the Big Bang boundary can be treated as a surface-like frame interface. The word "surface" should be read broadly. It may mean a conformal boundary, a quantum transition hypersurface, a limiting frame domain, or an abstract boundary in the space of admissible physical descriptions.

Define a cosmological boundary frame schematically as:

```text
BigBangBoundary = {
  preBoundaryDomain,
  postBoundaryDomain,
  boundarySurface,
  admissibleAspects,
  erasedAspects,
  transformedAspects,
  inheritedConstraints,
  entropyCondition,
  geometricTransition,
  fieldTransition
}
```

The boundary frame is not an observer. It is a physical admissibility interface. It governs which aspects can be represented in the post-boundary universe.

The boundary may erase ordinary records, transform field configurations, impose geometric constraints, preserve statistical patterns, or make some pre-boundary aspects permanently inaccessible.

This gives a general form:

$$
B:\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{pre}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{post}},
$$

where $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{pre}}$ is the set of pre-boundary or trans-boundary aspects, and $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{post}}$ is the set of post-boundary bindable aspects.

For an aspect $a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{pre}}$,

$$
B(a)\in
\{\text{erased},\text{transformed},\text{inherited constraint},\text{seeded perturbation},\text{field bias},\text{inaccessible}\}.
$$

This boundary map is not yet a physical law. It is a scaffold for asking what a mature theory would need to specify.

## 5. Boundary-Selective Frame Inheritance

The key Frames Theory concept is boundary-selective frame inheritance. Not every aspect of a prior or pre-classical domain can survive into the present universe. The boundary is an extreme admissibility filter.

A schematic survival condition is:

```text
survivesBoundary(aspect) =
  compatibleWithBoundaryDynamics(aspect)
  && not erasedByRecordDilution(aspect)
  && representableInPostBoundaryFields(aspect)
  && leavesRecordLikeConstraint(aspect)
```

This is the cosmological version of aspect-selective binding. Exposure to a boundary is not inheritance. Inheritance requires compatibility with the boundary's admissibility conditions.

Possible inherited aspects include:

- conformal structure;
- statistical correlations;
- field-frame biases;
- quantum-geometric constraints;
- topology or large-scale correlation patterns;
- low-record boundary conditions;
- primordial perturbation structure;
- asymmetries relevant to matter-antimatter imbalance.

Possible erased aspects include:

- ordinary macroscopic records;
- bound material structures;
- local biological or technological histories;
- massive clocks and rods from a prior aeon;
- most thermodynamic details of earlier matter organization.

This is not a claim that some specific previous-universe object survives. It is almost the opposite. Ordinary objects and records are precisely what the boundary should destroy or render inaccessible. What may survive is more abstract: constraints, admissible patterns, statistical residues, or field/geometric biases.

## 6. Pre-Boundary Smoothing and the Heat-Death Frame

A central motivation for inflation is the apparent smoothness, flatness, and large-scale uniformity of the early universe. Inflation explains this by introducing a rapid early expansion driven by an inflaton-like scalar field. Frames Theory should not treat that as the default explanation. It should ask whether smoothing may occur before the Big Bang boundary.

In a Penrose-compatible aeonic picture, the previous aeon approaches heat death. Black holes eventually evaporate. Massive particles decay or become irrelevant. Bound structures disappear. Ordinary clocks, rods, and material records lose operational meaning. What remains is increasingly radiation-like, dilute, and conformally describable.

Frames Theory interprets this as pre-boundary record dilution. The previous aeon's late state may be thermodynamically old, but it is also record-thinned from the perspective of what can pass through a conformal or cosmological boundary.

The sequence is:

```text
record-rich matter-structured aeon
-> expanding, cooling, diluting aeon
-> decay of massive structures and records
-> radiation-dominated or conformally admissible terminal frame
-> boundary surface
-> low-record initial current aeon
```

In this account, smoothing is not necessarily a post-boundary process. The boundary receives an already smoothed or scale-erased condition from the terminal previous aeon.

This can be summarized as:

$$
\text{smoothing}
\ne
\text{post-boundary inflaton repair},
$$

but rather

$$
\text{smoothing}=\text{pre-boundary record dilution plus conformal admissibility}.
$$

This is one of the strongest alternatives Frames Theory can offer. The early universe may appear smooth not because an arbitrary initial state was dynamically repaired after the boundary, but because the boundary itself is inherited from a previous frame whose late-time state has lost scale-bearing irregularity.

## 7. Boundary-Conditioned Observables Without an Inflaton Primitive

Frames Theory should not simply accept an inflaton as an explanatory primitive. Penrose has objected strongly to inflationary reasoning because it can appear to move the specialness of the early universe into an assumed scalar field and potential rather than explaining the low-entropy boundary itself. One need not adopt every Penrose claim to take this concern seriously.

The Frames Theory alternative is a boundary-conditioned observable model. The large-scale observables of the universe are treated as records of boundary admissibility, inherited constraints, and post-boundary frame differentiation.

Inflation is then one model in the comparison set, not the default engine of explanation.

The observables requiring explanation include:

- large-scale homogeneity;
- near spatial flatness;
- low-entropy early condition;
- primordial perturbation spectrum;
- cosmic microwave background anisotropies;
- primordial gravitational-wave constraints or signatures;
- matter-antimatter asymmetry;
- possible CP-violating anomalies;
- large-scale topology or correlation anomalies.

A Frames-Theoretic mapping is:

```text
Homogeneity:
  inherited from a boundary frame already smoothed by pre-boundary record dilution.

Flatness:
  an admissible post-boundary geometric condition, not necessarily an inflaton product.

Low entropy:
  a low-record initial condition for the current aeon, despite possible high entropy in a previous aeon.

Perturbations:
  boundary-compatible variations in field-frame or geometric inheritance.

CMB anisotropies:
  early post-boundary records of frame differentiation.

Matter-antimatter asymmetry:
  aspect-asymmetric field-frame binding during early field differentiation.

CP anomalies:
  possible field-frame bias, if confirmed and reconciled with other records.
```

The core claim is not that inflation is false. It is that Frames Theory should not need inflation as a primitive assumption. The deeper question is whether boundary conditions and frame inheritance can explain the observables inflation was designed to address.

## 8. Records Without Ordinary Memory

The Big Bang boundary cannot preserve ordinary memory. It cannot carry notebooks, organisms, planets, detector logs, or ordinary thermodynamic archives from a previous aeon into the current one. If cross-boundary records exist, they are not memories in the ordinary sense.

Frames Theory therefore distinguishes ordinary records from constraint-like records.

An ordinary record is a stable trace accessible within a frame network: a written mark, detector state, fossil, thermal trace, or memory. A constraint-like record is different. It is a boundary-conditioned structure that shapes later possibilities without preserving a readable historical archive.

Examples of possible constraint-like records include:

- statistical patterns in the cosmic microwave background;
- primordial gravitational-wave backgrounds;
- large-scale correlation anomalies;
- topology or curvature constraints;
- matter-antimatter asymmetry;
- CP-violating field-frame biases;
- low-entropy or low-record boundary conditions.

The early universe may therefore be record-poor but constraint-rich. It lacks internal historical records, but it is governed by strong boundary conditions that shape all later record formation.

This helps resolve an apparent paradox. A previous aeon may be old and high entropy in its own frame, while the new aeon begins with few accessible records. The boundary acts as a record filter:

$$
\text{previous-aeon entropy}
\not\equiv
\text{current-aeon record accessibility}.
$$

The arrow of time in the current aeon may then arise from the growth of records away from the boundary.

## 9. Quantum Fields Across a Cosmological Boundary

Paper 12 argued that quantum fields can be modeled as persistent field-frames and particles as excitation-aspects. A cosmological boundary raises the question of whether field-frames themselves can persist, transform, or be reinitialized across an aeonic boundary.

Frames Theory should be careful here. It should not say that an ordinary field simply passes unchanged through the Big Bang. Instead, it should ask which field-frame aspects are boundary-admissible.

Possible field-frame inheritances include:

- vacuum-sector constraints;
- symmetry-breaking biases;
- coupling-structure residues;
- CP-violating phase biases;
- statistical correlations among field modes;
- conformally admissible radiation patterns.

In this language, an anomalous particle decay result would not automatically prove pre-Big-Bang physics. It would be a local field-frame record requiring reconciliation. But if a CP-violating anomaly survives experimental and theoretical scrutiny, Frames Theory could interpret it as a candidate field-frame bias:

$$
\mu(\text{decay channel})
\ne
\mu(\text{CP-conjugate channel}).
$$

A cosmological interpretation would require showing that such a bias is connected to a boundary inheritance mechanism:

```text
pre-boundary field-frame condition
-> boundary admissibility filter
-> post-boundary field-frame bias
-> decay-channel asymmetry
-> detector records
```

This is a long evidentiary chain. Frames Theory should treat it as a hypothesis requiring many layers of reconciliation, not as an immediate conclusion.

## 10. CP Violation, Matter-Antimatter Asymmetry, and Field-Frame Bias

CP violation is one of the most important clues in cosmology and particle physics because the observable universe contains far more matter than antimatter. Known Standard Model CP violation exists, especially in quark mixing, but it appears insufficient by itself to explain the observed matter-antimatter asymmetry.

Frames Theory interprets CP violation as aspect-asymmetric field-frame binding. A process and its CP-conjugate process do not bind with identical measures:

$$
\mu(E\to f)
\ne
\mu(CP(E)\to CP(f)).
$$

In early-universe contexts, such asymmetry may contribute to baryogenesis or leptogenesis. In a boundary-frame context, one may ask whether the asymmetry reflects a post-boundary dynamical process, an inherited boundary condition, or both.

This paper does not claim that observed CP violation proves a previous aeon. It suggests a frame-theoretic way to classify the possibilities:

```text
Standard Model frame:
  CP violation arises from known mixing and phase structure.

Beyond-Standard-Model frame:
  additional fields or couplings modify asymmetry.

Boundary-inheritance frame:
  field-frame bias is inherited or selected at the cosmological boundary.

Detector-record frame:
  measured asymmetries must survive experimental reconciliation.
```

A boundary-inheritance account becomes stronger only if it reconciles particle-physics records, cosmological asymmetry, quantum-field dynamics, and boundary cosmology better than alternatives.

## 11. Penrose CCC as Conformal Frame Transition

Conformal cyclic cosmology proposes that the remote future of one aeon can be conformally matched to the Big Bang of the next. The key idea is that when mass becomes irrelevant in the far future, scale loses operational meaning. The infinite future of one aeon may then be conformally equivalent to the beginning of another.

Frames Theory can interpret each aeon as a maximal cosmological frame:

$$
\mathcal{A}_n
\rightarrow
\partial\mathcal{A}_{n,n+1}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{A}_{n+1}.
$$

Here $\partial\mathcal{A}_{n,n+1}$ is the conformal boundary frame between aeons.

In this picture, the boundary is not a singularity. It is a surface-like frame interface. Smoothing occurs because the previous aeon has approached heat death and has lost mass-bearing, scale-defining structure. The boundary does not need to inflate away arbitrary irregularity; it inherits a conformally thinned condition.

Frames Theory does not need to endorse every specific CCC prediction. It can use CCC as a model of how aeonic frame inheritance might work:

- ordinary records are erased;
- mass-dependent structure loses meaning;
- conformally admissible aspects may survive;
- the next aeon begins as a low-record frame;
- possible observable residues must appear as current-aeon records.

This makes CCC especially interesting for Frames Theory because it gives a concrete alternative to singular origin and post-boundary inflation.

## 12. Loop Quantum Cosmology as Quantum-Geometric Frame Transition

Loop quantum cosmology and related loop quantum gravity approaches offer a different non-singular picture. Rather than a conformal transition between aeons, the Big Bang may be replaced by a quantum bounce. A prior contracting phase transitions through a high-curvature quantum-geometric regime into the expanding universe.

Frames Theory can interpret this as a quantum-geometric frame transition:

```text
pre-bounce frame network
-> quantum-geometric transition frame
-> post-bounce frame network
```

The underlying structures are not ordinary fields on a fixed background. They are quantum-geometric structures such as spin networks, spin foams, holonomies, and quantized areas or volumes.

A Frames Theory mapping is:

```text
spin network = pre-spacetime frame graph
spin foam = transition history of quantum geometry frames
area/volume eigenvalues = quantized geometric frame capacities
holonomies = relational transport between frame nodes
bounce = high-curvature frame-boundary reconciliation event
```

In this model, the boundary's admissibility filter is not conformal heat-death smoothing. It is quantum-geometric transition dynamics. Some aspects may pass through the bounce, others may be erased or transformed.

Frames Theory can therefore compare CCC and loop quantum cosmology without collapsing them into one another:

```text
CCC:
  boundary admissibility is conformal and aeonic.

Loop quantum cosmology:
  boundary admissibility is quantum-geometric and bounce-like.

Frames Theory:
  both are models of cross-boundary frame inheritance under different admissibility rules.
```

## 13. Entropy, Time, and the Growth of Records

Frames Theory treats time through update order, memory, and records. The past is not a perfect global archive. It is the record-accessible past of a frame network.

The Big Bang boundary is therefore central to the arrow of time. The early universe appears to be a low-entropy boundary condition, or at least a low-record condition from the perspective of the current aeon. Later cosmic history produces increasing records: nuclei, atoms, stars, galaxies, planets, fossils, detectors, databases, and observers.

In the frame-theoretic picture:

$$
\text{time arrow}=\text{direction of increasing record accessibility from the boundary}.
$$

This does not require that the boundary contain a rich memory of the previous aeon. In fact, the opposite may be true. The boundary may be record-poor precisely because ordinary records have been erased, diluted, or rendered non-admissible.

The previous aeon may be thermodynamically old. The current aeon may be record-young. These are not the same statement, because entropy and record-accessibility are frame-relative.

This gives Frames Theory a way to express a subtle possibility:

> The current universe begins not from nothing, but from a boundary that is poor in ordinary records and rich in admissibility constraints.

## 14. Observational Reconciliation and Evidence Standards

A cross-boundary hypothesis must meet a high evidentiary standard. Frames Theory should not treat every anomaly as evidence for a previous aeon, a bounce, or inherited field structure.

A hypothesis becomes stronger when it reconciles multiple record families:

- cosmic microwave background records;
- large-scale structure data;
- primordial gravitational-wave constraints;
- particle-physics decay and CP-violation records;
- matter-antimatter asymmetry;
- black-hole and gravitational records;
- quantum-gravity model constraints;
- entropy and arrow-of-time arguments.

In Frames Theory language, a proposed cross-boundary feature must become objective through redundant consistency across frames. It is not enough for one dataset to admit an exotic interpretation. The feature must stabilize across independent observational and theoretical neighborhoods.

This principle can be written schematically as:

$$
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{boundary}}(h)
\sim
R_{\mathrm{CMB}}(h)
\cdot
R_{\mathrm{QFT}}(h)
\cdot
R_{\mathrm{QG}}(h)
\cdot
K_{\mathrm{cross}}(h),
$$

where $h$ is a cross-boundary hypothesis, $R$ terms represent record support in different domains, and $K_{\mathrm{cross}}$ represents consistency across those domains.

This expression is only schematic. Its purpose is to emphasize that evidence for boundary inheritance must be networked, not isolated.

## 16. Limitations and Open Problems

This paper is exploratory. It does not prove conformal cyclic cosmology. It does not prove loop quantum cosmology. It does not refute inflation. It does not derive the Big Bang from Frames Theory. It does not claim that CP anomalies, CMB anomalies, or large-scale structures are automatically evidence of a previous aeon.

Its more modest claim is that Frames Theory provides a disciplined language for boundary accessibility, selective inheritance, record erasure, and post-boundary observable constraints.

Several open problems remain.

First, the boundary map $B$ must be given real dynamics. Without a concrete theory of the boundary, the formalism remains schematic.

Second, the relation between conformal inheritance and quantum field-frame inheritance must be clarified. Which field aspects are truly conformally admissible?

Third, loop quantum cosmology and CCC are technically different. Frames Theory must not blur them into a single vague pre-Big-Bang picture.

Fourth, a non-inflationary boundary-conditioned observable model must explain the same data inflation is usually invoked to explain: homogeneity, flatness, perturbations, CMB structure, and gravitational-wave constraints.

Fifth, entropy and record-accessibility need a sharper mathematical relation. A previous aeon can be high-entropy while the current aeon is low-record, but the formal bridge needs work.

Sixth, observational standards must remain strict. Exotic cosmological inheritance should be accepted only when it improves cross-frame consistency across many independent records.

These limitations define the next stage of the research program.

## 17. Conclusion

The Big Bang should not be treated as a literal singular object in Frames Theory. A singularity marks the breakdown of a description, not the foundation of ontology. Once the singularity is discarded, the Big Bang becomes a boundary problem.

This paper proposed that the Big Bang is a cosmological admissibility boundary: a frame-boundary surface or transition interface that determines which aspects can become time-bearing, record-bearing, field-bearing, and geometry-bearing in the current universe.

The key proposal is that smoothing need not be a post-boundary inflaton-driven process. In a Penrose-compatible aeonic reading, the previous aeon approaches heat death, loses mass-bearing structure, dilutes ordinary records, and becomes conformally admissible. The current aeon's early smoothness may therefore be inherited from pre-boundary record dilution and scale erasure, rather than produced by a scalar-field smoothing phase after the boundary.

Frames Theory can compare several possibilities:

$$
\text{CCC}
=
\text{conformal aeonic boundary},
$$

$$
\text{loop quantum cosmology}
=
\text{quantum-geometric bounce boundary},
$$

$$
\text{inflation}
=
\text{post-boundary smoothing model},
$$

$$
\text{Frames Theory}
=
\text{boundary-conditioned frame inheritance and record formation}.
$$

The result is a non-singular, non-inflaton-primitive way to frame the Big Bang. The observable universe is not simply what appears after a beginning from nothing. It is a post-boundary frame whose records, fields, geometry, and arrow of time are shaped by what the cosmological boundary admits, erases, transforms, and allows to become real for this aeon.
