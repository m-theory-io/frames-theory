# Cosmological Frames and Horizon-Bounded Reality in Frames Theory

## Abstract

Cosmology forces the problem of bounded information to its largest scale. Every observer is embedded inside the universe, limited by finite age, finite signal speed, expanding spacetime, and horizons. No physical frame can occupy an external position from which the entire universe is operationally available at once. Frames Theory interprets this not as a practical inconvenience, but as an ontological clue: cosmological reality is accessed through causal patches, horizon-bounded domains, and consistency relations among overlapping frames. This paper develops the cosmological extension of Frames Theory. A cosmological frame is a large-scale bounded informational domain whose state includes accessible matter, radiation, geometry, records, and horizon-limited correlations. The observable universe is not the universe as seen from nowhere; it is the maximal region available to a class of embedded frames. Regions beyond a horizon may be included in mathematical models, but they cannot enter a frame's operational reality without causal contact. This paper does not deny global cosmological modeling, nor does it claim to solve quantum gravity or derive spacetime. It argues that cosmology should be interpreted through horizon-bounded frame access: reality is globally modeled, but locally and cosmologically accessed.

## 1. Introduction

The previous paper extended Frames Theory into relativistic causal structure. It argued that a relativistic frame is a bounded informational domain whose accessible state is constrained by light cones, causal diamonds, horizons, and finite signal propagation.

Cosmology is the next natural step. Relativity already denies a universal present. Cosmology adds something even stronger: some regions may never become accessible to a given embedded frame. An observer's description of the universe is limited by particle horizons, event horizons, finite measurement records, and the fact that the observer is inside the system being described.

This raises a foundational question:

> What does it mean to describe the universe when every physical describer is bounded by a horizon-limited frame?

Frames Theory answers by treating cosmological descriptions as frame-relative but consistency-constrained. A global cosmological model may be mathematically useful. It may represent spacetime regions, fields, and histories far beyond what any observer can directly access. But the operational content of such a model is always mediated by embedded frames with finite causal access.

The central thesis of this paper is:

> Cosmological reality is horizon-bounded for embedded frames: each cosmological frame accesses a causal patch, while global models remain consistency-constrained extrapolations beyond any single frame's operational domain.

This thesis is not a rejection of cosmology. It is a discipline for interpreting cosmological knowledge. Frames Theory does not say that only what is observed exists. It says that physical description must distinguish what is operationally accessible, what is inferred through consistency, and what is mathematically modeled beyond any possible frame access.

## 2. Cosmological Frames

A cosmological frame is a large-scale bounded informational domain associated with an embedded observer, observer community, causal patch, or horizon-limited region of spacetime.

Building on the relativistic frame notation, write

$$
F_{\mathrm{cos}}=(\rho_{\mathrm{cos}},N_{\mathrm{cos}},C_{\mathrm{cos}},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cos}}),
$$

where $\rho_{\mathrm{cos}}$ is the accessible cosmological state, $N_{\mathrm{cos}}$ is the set of accessible neighboring frames or regions, $C_{\mathrm{cos}}$ is the set of consistency constraints, and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cos}}$ is the causal domain of the cosmological frame.

The state $\rho_{\mathrm{cos}}$ may include many kinds of information:

1. Local records, instruments, and observational archives.
2. Incoming photons, neutrinos, gravitational waves, and other messengers.
3. Large-scale structure inferred from surveys.
4. Cosmic microwave background records.
5. Dynamical models of expansion history.
6. Boundary assumptions about inaccessible regions.
7. Theoretical constraints from general relativity, quantum field theory, and thermodynamics.

This state is not a direct inventory of the universe as a whole. It is the state available to a bounded cosmological frame.

Frames Theory therefore distinguishes:

**Accessible cosmological reality.** The portion of cosmological structure from which records can enter the frame.

**Inferential cosmological reality.** Structure not directly observed but constrained by patterns within the accessible domain.

**Modeled global reality.** Mathematical extension beyond the frame's causal access, useful for theory but not operationally available as a record.

These distinctions help prevent a common ambiguity. When cosmologists speak of "the universe," they may mean the observable universe, a larger spacetime manifold, a statistical ensemble of possible regions, or a theoretical global solution. Frames Theory asks which frame has access to which content.

## 3. Particle Horizons and Event Horizons

Cosmology contains more than one kind of horizon. A particle horizon marks the boundary of what could have influenced an observer since the beginning of the relevant cosmological era. It is a limit on past accessibility. A cosmological event horizon marks the boundary of what can ever influence an observer in the future. It is a limit on eventual accessibility.

Frames Theory interprets these as different causal information boundaries.

The particle horizon limits what can currently be included in the frame's observational past:

$$
p\notin J^-(F_{\mathrm{now}})
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
p\notin \mathrm{CurrentAccess}(F_{\mathrm{cos}}).
$$

The event horizon limits what can ever become part of the frame's accessible future:

$$
p\notin J^-_{\mathrm{future}}(F_{\mathrm{cos}})
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
p\notin \mathrm{EventualAccess}(F_{\mathrm{cos}}).
$$

The notation is schematic, but the distinction is important. Some information is currently inaccessible but may become accessible later. Other information is permanently inaccessible to the frame because cosmic expansion prevents any future signal exchange.

This means that a cosmological frame has layered boundaries:

1. **Current observational boundary:** what records have already arrived.
2. **Eventual causal boundary:** what records could ever arrive.
3. **Model boundary:** what the theory represents beyond possible access.

The observable universe is tied to the first two. A global cosmological model may extend into the third.

## 4. The Observable Universe as a Frame-Relative Domain

The observable universe is often described as if it were simply "the universe we can see." Frames Theory sharpens this statement. The observable universe is the maximal record-bearing region available to a cosmological frame at a given stage of its history.

It is not the whole universe in an absolute sense. Nor is it merely subjective. It is an objective causal domain relative to a class of embedded frames sharing similar location, motion, and observational history.

For a cosmological frame $F_i$, write the observable domain as

$$
\mathcal{O}(F_i,t)
\subseteq
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}},
$$

where $\mathcal{O}(F_i,t)$ is the region whose signals have become accessible to $F_i$ by time $t$.

Different cosmological frames may have different observable domains:

$$
\mathcal{O}(F_i,t)
\ne
\mathcal{O}(F_j,t)
$$

when their worldlines, horizons, or causal histories differ.

This does not make cosmology arbitrary. Overlapping observable domains must reconcile where they share records. If two cosmological frames can compare observations of the same region, their descriptions must satisfy consistency constraints. But where no overlap or causal connection exists, no direct reconciliation is possible.

Thus, the observable universe is frame-relative in access but objective in structure. It is not private imagination. It is a physically bounded domain of records.

## 5. Horizon-Bounded Reality

A horizon-bounded reality is the reality operationally available to a frame whose causal access is limited by horizons.

This phrase should be handled carefully. It does not mean that inaccessible regions are unreal. It means that they do not participate in the frame's reality through direct record, observation, or reconciliation.

Frames Theory therefore separates three claims:

**Ontological humility.** There may be structure beyond any given horizon.

**Operational boundedness.** No embedded frame can access or verify all such structure.

**Consistency discipline.** Claims about inaccessible structure must be constrained by the accessible records and the theory linking them.

The frame-relative cosmological state may be written as a restriction of a broader modeled state:

$$
\rho_{F_i}=\mathrm{Restrict}_{\mathcal{D}_i}(\rho_{\mathrm{model}}),
$$

or, when inaccessible degrees of freedom are entangled with accessible ones, schematically as

$$
\rho_{F_i}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{\mathcal{D}_i}}(\rho_{\mathrm{model}}).
$$

Here $\overline{\mathcal{D}_i}$ denotes degrees of freedom outside the frame's causal domain. These formulas are not a completed cosmological quantum formalism. They express the same idea used in the state-update and relativistic-frame papers: a reduced state is what a larger structure looks like from within a bounded frame.

Horizon-bounded reality is therefore not anti-realist. It is relational realism under causal constraints.

## 6. Overlapping Patches and Cosmological Consistency

If cosmological frames are patch-relative, one might worry that reality fragments into disconnected perspectives. Frames Theory avoids this by emphasizing overlap and consistency.

Two cosmological frames $F_i$ and $F_j$ may have observable domains that overlap:

$$
\mathcal{O}(F_i)\cap\mathcal{O}(F_j)\ne\varnothing.
$$

Where they overlap, their records and inferred descriptions must be mutually reconcilable. A consistency functional can be written in the same spirit as the first Frames Theory paper:

$$
\mathcal{K}(F_i,F_j)=\exp\left(-D(\rho_i^{\Omega},\rho_j^{\Omega})\right),
$$

where $\Omega$ is the shared observational domain and $D$ is an appropriate information distance.

If the frames share many records and the distance between their restricted descriptions is small, then $\mathcal{K}\approx 1$. If their accessible domains do not overlap, or if no records can be exchanged, then direct consistency cannot be established operationally.

This suggests a patchwork view of cosmological objectivity. Cosmological facts become objective across connected regions of the frame network when records can be shared, compared, and reconciled. Where horizons prevent all such sharing, objectivity cannot be extended by observation. It can only be extended by theoretical modeling.

Thus the global universe, if represented, is not constructed by direct access. It is stitched together through overlap, inference, symmetry assumptions, dynamical laws, and boundary conditions.

## 7. Cosmic Microwave Background as Shared Record

The cosmic microwave background provides a useful example of cosmological frame-binding. It is not merely light arriving from far away. It is a large-scale fossil record from the early universe, accessible to many observer frames within our causal domain.

In Frames Theory language, the CMB functions as a shared cosmological record. Different detectors, satellites, laboratories, and scientific communities bind to the same incoming radiation field through different instruments and analyses. Their records become consistent through calibration, comparison, and theoretical modeling.

The CMB is objective within our cosmological frame network because it is:

1. **Redundant:** many instruments can sample it.
2. **Stable:** its statistical structure persists across observations.
3. **Shared:** many frames can compare records.
4. **Model-linked:** it constrains a common cosmological history.

Yet the CMB is also horizon-bounded. It comes from a last-scattering surface within our observable domain. It does not give direct access to all regions of a possibly larger universe. It is a powerful record, but still a frame-relative record.

This example shows how Frames Theory can respect cosmological objectivity without treating cosmological observation as God's-eye access. The CMB is real, public, and scientifically objective, but its objectivity is achieved through redundant frame-binding within a causal domain.

## 8. Inflation, Extrapolation, and Inaccessible Domains

Inflationary cosmology often suggests that the universe extends far beyond the observable region. It may contain domains that are permanently inaccessible to us. Some models also suggest a multiverse or a landscape of causally disconnected regions.

Frames Theory does not reject such models. It asks what kind of claim is being made.

A claim about an inaccessible domain may be:

1. A consequence of a model fitted to accessible records.
2. A statistical extrapolation from observed structure.
3. A boundary condition imposed on the global solution.
4. A speculative extension beyond direct operational test.

These are not equivalent. Frames Theory encourages explicit labeling of the epistemic and operational status of each claim.

If an inflationary model predicts correlations or signatures within our accessible domain, then those predictions can enter the frame network through observation. If it predicts only regions forever beyond causal access, then those regions remain model-mediated rather than record-mediated.

This does not make them meaningless. It does change their status. They are not part of the frame's directly bound reality. They are part of a broader theoretical structure constrained by what the frame can observe.

## 9. The Universe as Self-Containing Frame

Cosmology differs from ordinary laboratory physics because the observer is inside the system being modeled. There is no external apparatus outside the universe that can receive the universe as an object.

This raises the question of whether the universe itself can be a frame.

Frames Theory can treat a global universe model as a limiting or formal frame:

$$
F_{\mathrm{U}}=(\rho_{\mathrm{U}},N_{\mathrm{U}},C_{\mathrm{U}}),
$$

but this object is not a frame in the ordinary operational sense. It has no external neighbor from which a complete outside observation can arrive. If $F_{\mathrm{U}}$ contains all physical frames, then there is no external receiving frame to bind to it as a whole.

This creates a self-reference problem. Embedded frames can model the universe, but their models are themselves physical records inside the universe. The map is part of the territory.

Frames Theory therefore distinguishes:

**Operational frames:** bounded systems that exchange information with other frames.

**Cosmological patch frames:** large-scale causal domains accessible to embedded observers.

**Formal global frames:** mathematical representations of the universe as a whole, not directly accessible from outside.

The global universe may be represented mathematically, but no embedded frame can operationally complete that representation as a total record. This is not a proof that global description is impossible. It is a warning that global description has a different status from ordinary frame-to-frame observation.

## 10. Horizon Entropy and Information Bounds

Horizons are not only causal boundaries. In gravitational physics, horizons are associated with entropy and information bounds. Black hole horizons have Bekenstein-Hawking entropy proportional to area:

$$
S_{\mathrm{BH}}=\frac{k_B c^3 A}{4G\hbar}.
$$

Cosmological horizons, especially in de Sitter-like spacetimes, are also associated with horizon temperature and entropy. These results suggest that causal boundaries may carry finite informational capacity.

Frames Theory interprets this cautiously. Horizon entropy may indicate that the information accessible to a horizon-bounded frame is finite or effectively bounded by boundary structure. This resonates with the frame-theoretic idea that reality is accessed through bounded informational domains.

However, Frames Theory does not derive horizon entropy in this paper. Nor does it claim that holography follows from the frame formalism. The point is structural: gravitational horizons already connect causal access, thermodynamics, and information. That makes them natural candidates for future Frames Theory development.

A possible future direction is to relate frame accessibility to entropy bounds:

$$
\dim \mathcal{H}_{F_i}
\lesssim
\exp\left(\frac{A_i}{4\ell_P^2}\right),
$$

where $A_i$ is an area associated with the boundary of the frame's causal domain. This is only a research hint, not a result established here.

## 11. Cosmological Objectivity

Cosmological objectivity is not the same as local laboratory objectivity. A laboratory result can often be repeated, recorded, and inspected by many nearby frames. Cosmological facts often concern unique events, ancient light, inaccessible regions, or large-scale statistical patterns.

Frames Theory interprets cosmological objectivity as high consistency across many independent records within a causal patch.

For example, the expansion history of the universe is not inferred from a single record. It is constrained by galaxy redshifts, supernova observations, baryon acoustic oscillations, CMB anisotropies, gravitational lensing, nucleosynthesis, and structure formation. These records enter the cosmological frame through different channels but must reconcile into a shared model.

Cosmological objectivity therefore depends on:

1. Diverse record channels.
2. Cross-calibration among instruments.
3. Consistency across independent observables.
4. Robustness under model variation.
5. Compatibility with local physics.

This is redundant frame-binding at cosmological scale. The universe becomes objective to us not by being viewed from outside, but by many internal records mutually constraining a coherent description.

## 13. Relation to Existing Approaches

### 13.1 Observational Cosmology

Observational cosmology already works with finite records: light cones, surveys, redshifts, background radiation, and instrument-limited data. Frames Theory provides an ontology for this practice. It treats the observable universe as a frame-relative causal domain rather than as an unbounded view from nowhere.

### 13.2 Causal Patch Cosmology

Causal patch approaches emphasize the physics accessible within a single causal domain. Frames Theory is naturally aligned with this emphasis. It adds a broader language of frames, record binding, consistency, and horizon-bounded objectivity.

### 13.3 Black Hole Complementarity

Black hole complementarity suggests that different observers may have different but noncontradictory descriptions of information near horizons. Frames Theory resonates with this idea because it treats horizon-bounded descriptions as frame-relative and consistency-constrained.

### 13.4 Holography

Holography suggests that information in a region may be encoded on a boundary. Frames Theory does not derive holography, but it treats horizon boundaries as natural places where causal access, information capacity, and frame description meet.

### 13.5 Relational and Perspectival Cosmology

Relational approaches emphasize that cosmological description is made by observers within the universe. Frames Theory agrees, while trying to make the relevant structures more explicit: causal domains, information transfer, record accessibility, consistency, and self-containing description.

## 14. Limitations and Open Problems

This paper is conceptual and interpretive. It does not derive the Friedmann equations, inflationary dynamics, dark energy, quantum gravity, holography, or horizon entropy.

It does not decide whether inaccessible regions exist in a strong ontological sense. It distinguishes operational access, theoretical inference, and mathematical modeling.

It does not provide a complete measure of cosmological frame consistency. A future treatment should specify distance measures among cosmological records, likelihood structures, and model-dependent consistency functionals.

It does not solve the measure problem in inflationary or multiverse cosmology. It only reframes such problems as issues involving horizon-bounded sampling and model-mediated extrapolation.

It does not claim that the universe is literally a single ordinary frame. It distinguishes operational frames, causal patch frames, and formal global frames.

Finally, it does not make new empirical predictions. Its contribution is to clarify the ontology of cosmological description under horizon-bounded access.

## 15. Conclusion

Cosmology makes the boundedness of physical description unavoidable. Every observer is inside the universe, limited by finite signal speed, finite cosmic age, expansion, horizons, and finite records. No embedded frame can convert the entire universe into an operationally available object.

Frames Theory interprets this as horizon-bounded reality. A cosmological frame accesses a causal patch. It forms objective descriptions through redundant records, overlapping observations, and consistency constraints. It may model regions beyond its horizon, but those regions have a different operational status from accessible records.

The compact statement is:

$$
\text{cosmological frame}=\text{horizon-bounded causal patch with accessible records}.
$$

This view preserves cosmological realism while rejecting the assumption that physical description begins from an impossible external standpoint. The universe can be modeled globally, but it is known, tested, and reconciled from within bounded frames.
